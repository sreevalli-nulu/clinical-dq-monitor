Anomaly model (evaluated on test data)

An Isolation Forest looks for visits and adverse-event records that are unusual compared with similar ones. To measure how well it works, the project made a copy of the trial data and hid known errors in it, so here we know the right answers. These are test results, not findings about the real trial.

How to read this dashboard

Detect with code, explain with words

Rules, z-scores and the anomaly model decide what is flagged and how severe it is. The explanation layer only turns those facts into plain language. It cannot change a severity, and a card is replaced by plain template wording if its text mentions a number that is not in the evidence or uses accusing words.

Z-scores and the colours

Red means a site is higher than the other sites, blue means lower. Watch is |z| of 2 or more, Alert is 3 or more. Which direction matters depends on the indicator: a low adverse-event rate can mean under-reporting, while for digit preference only a high share is a concern.

Some flags are chance

Every site is tested on several indicators. At the Watch level about 1 test in 22 flags by pure chance, so with dozens of tests a few flags are expected even when nothing is wrong. Treat a single Watch as a reason to look, not as evidence.

Small sites

A site with fewer than 5 dosed patients is marked too few patients and gets no z-score. A rate from one or two people tells us nothing.

The data

CDISC Pilot 01, a public sample clinical-trial dataset in the SDTM format. It is a teaching dataset, not a live study.

What this is not

This is a learning project. It is not validated software and must not be used for any clinical, regulatory or monitoring decision.
